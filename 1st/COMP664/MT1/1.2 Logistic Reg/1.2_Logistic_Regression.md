# 1.2 Logistic Regression

## Binary logistic regression

For features \(x_i\) and label \(y_i\in\{0,1\}\), the model defines a logit \(z_i=w^\top x_i+b\) and a probability \(p_i=\sigma(z_i)\), where

\[
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}.
\]

The probabilities of classes 1 and 0 are \(p_i\) and \(1-p_i\), respectively. With a threshold of \(0.5\), the decision boundary is \(w^\top x_i+b=0\).

Assuming conditionally independent examples, the likelihood is

\[
P(y\mid X,w,b)=\prod_{i=1}^N p_i^{y_i}(1-p_i)^{1-y_i}.
\]

Taking its negative logarithm gives binary cross-entropy:

\[
L=-\sum_{i=1}^N[y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)].
\]

Using \(\sigma'(z_i)=p_i(1-p_i)\) gives \(\partial L/\partial z_i=p_i-y_i\). The weight and bias gradients are

\[
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_j}=\sum_i(p_i-y_i)x_{ij},
\qquad
\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}=\sum_i(p_i-y_i).
\]

For mean cross-entropy, divide both expressions by \(N\). The negative gradient uses \(y_i-p_i\), which is the signed error in the direction of the update.

## Multiclass logistic regression

Give each of \(C\) classes its own weight vector. For example \(i\), class \(c\) has logit \(s_{ic}=w_c^\top x_i+b_c\) and softmax probability

\[
p_{ic}=\frac{e^{s_{ic}}}{\sum_{k=1}^C e^{s_{ik}}}.
\]

The probabilities sum to one across classes. Scores \((5,4,3)\) yield approximately \((0.665,0.245,0.090)\). Subtracting the largest score before exponentiating preserves the distribution and keeps the exponentials numerically stable.

With one-hot labels \(y_{ic}\), multiclass cross-entropy is \(L=-\sum_i\sum_c y_{ic}\log p_{ic}\). Each example contributes \(-\log p_{i,y_i}\), the negative log probability of its true class. The logit gradient is \(p_{ic}-y_{ic}\). Section 1.4 derives this result from the softmax Jacobian.
